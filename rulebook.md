Chapter 1: General Provisions, Governance, and Academic Structure

Section 1.1: Statutory Authority and Scope

1.  These Academic and Administrative Regulations (hereinafter referred to as
    the "Rulebook") are established under the statutory authority vested in the
    University Senate, the Academic Council, and the Board of Regents.
2.  The rules, protocols, mandates, and procedural policies detailed herein
    apply to all matriculated undergraduate, postgraduate, and doctoral
    students, whether enrolled on a full-time, part-time, transient, or
    non-degree basis across all schools, faculties, institutes, centers, and
    residential housing facilities operated by the University.
3.  Every student, upon accepting admission and completing registration, enters
    into a binding covenant with the University and agrees to abide by all
    institutional rules, disciplinary codes, academic performance thresholds,
    and financial regulations set forth in this document or duly amended by the
    Office of the Academic Registrar.
4.  Ignorance of any provision, code, deadline, or requirement contained within
    this Rulebook shall under no circumstances be admitted as a defense,
    mitigating circumstance, or justification in any disciplinary inquiry,
    appeal, or academic evaluation.

Section 1.2: Academic Calendar, Term Structures, and Unit Quantification

1.  The standard academic year is divided into two primary semesters—the Fall
    Semester and the Spring Semester—each comprising a minimum of fifteen (15)
    weeks of direct instructional delivery, exclusive of orientation periods,
    recess breaks, study recesses, and formal semester examination windows.
2.  An optional Summer Term comprising seven (7) intensive instructional weeks
    may be scheduled at the sole discretion of the Academic Council to
    facilitate remedial coursework, academic acceleration, or specialized
    clinical, practical, and research modules.
3.  Instructional workloads are quantified using the Standard University Credit
    Unit (SUCU). One lecture credit unit is equivalent to one (1) contact hour
    (defined as 50 minutes of structured instruction) per week over a 15-week
    semester. One laboratory, practical, or clinical credit unit represents a
    minimum of two to three (2–3) contact hours per week of supervised work over
    the same instructional duration.
4.  Full-time undergraduate students must enroll in a minimum course load of
    twelve (12) credit units and may not exceed an absolute maximum of
    twenty-one (21) credit units per semester, unless granted prior formal
    over-enrollment authorization pursuant to Section 2.4.

Section 1.3: Institutional Governance and Jurisdictional Hierarchies

1.  Academic governance is executed through the Office of the Academic
    Registrar, subject to the legislative oversight of the Academic Council, the
    University Senate, and the Executive Board of Regents.
2.  The hierarchy of institutional jurisdiction on academic matters is defined
    as follows:
      - Level I: Course Instructor / Module Coordinator
      - Level II: Department Chair / Head of Department (HoD)
      - Level III: Academic Dean of the Respective Faculty
      - Level IV: Academic Committee / Standing Senate Committee
      - Level V: University Senate and the Office of the Academic Registrar
      - Level VI: Board of Regents (Appellate Body of Ultimate Recourse)
3.  Any formal determination, procedural ruling, or disciplinary sanction
    rendered by a superior jurisdictional body automatically supersedes,
    abrogates, and replaces any conflicting decision rendered by a subordinate
    level, unless explicit provision to the contrary is made in this Rulebook.

Section 1.4: Official Communications, Notice Requirements, and Legal Delivery

1.  The University provides each matriculated student with an official
    institutional electronic mail address (@student.university.edu) upon
    registration.
2.  The institutional email account constitutes the sole official, legally
    recognized channel for all academic, administrative, disciplinary, and
    financial communications.
3.  Any notice, summons, grade disclosure, fee invoice, disciplinary accusation,
    or regulatory amendment transmitted to a student's institutional email
    address shall be deemed legally received, delivered, and acknowledged within
    twenty-four (24) hours of electronic dispatch by the University servers.
4.  Students are obligated to inspect their university email accounts on a daily
    basis during academic terms and at least once per calendar week during
    official recess periods. Failure to access, read, or manage mailboxes
    (including mailbox capacity overflows) shall not constitute grounds for
    waiver, extension, or appeal.

Section 1.5: Amendments, Precedence, and Severability

1.  The University Senate, with the concurrence of the Office of the Academic
    Registrar, reserves the express right to modify, amend, supplement, or
    repeal any section of this Rulebook at any regular or extraordinary meeting.
2.  Amendments shall take effect immediately upon official electronic
    dissemination to the university community, unless a specific prospective
    effective date is enacted by the Senate.
3.  If any section, paragraph, clause, sentence, or phrase of this Rulebook is
    declared invalid, illegal, unenforceable, or unconstitutional by a competent
    court of law or judicial authority, such determination shall not affect,
    impair, or invalidate the remaining portions, which shall remain in full
    force and effect.

Chapter 2: Admissions, Registration, and Enrollment Status

Section 2.1: Matriculation and Conditions of Final Enrolment

1.  Admission to any undergraduate, graduate, or professional degree program is
    provisional until the Office of the Academic Registrar verifies, receives,
    and authenticates all original educational transcripts, standardized
    examination scores, national identity verifications, and character
    certifications.
2.  All prospective candidates must submit verified physical or
    cryptographically validated digital copies of qualifying prerequisites prior
    to the conclusion of the fourth week of the initial semester of
    registration.
3.  Any discovery of forged, falsified, misstated, or artificially inflated
    credentials—regardless of whether the misstatement is detected prior to
    matriculation, during active studies, or subsequent to the conferral of a
    degree—shall result in immediate revocation of admission, retroactive
    cancellation of academic credits, permanent expulsion, and referral to
    criminal authorities.

Section 2.2: Course Registration, Adding, Dropping, and Waitlists

1.  Prior to the commencement of each semester, students must register for their
    designated lecture courses, laboratory modules, and elective offerings
    through the University Student Information System (SIS).
2.  The formal Add/Drop Period commences at 08:00 on the first day of classes
    and terminates precisely at 23:59 on the tenth (10th) calendar day of
    instructional activity. During this window, a student may add or drop
    courses without academic or transcript record penalties.
3.  Courses dropped during the formal Add/Drop Period shall not appear on the
    student's official or unofficial academic transcripts, and tuition fees
    shall be reconciled in accordance with the standard fee refund table
    outlined in Chapter 7.
4.  Students occupying a waitlist slot must monitor the SIS continuously; if a
    seat becomes vacant, an automated enrollment authorization is generated,
    requiring student electronic acceptance within twelve (12) hours, failing
    which the seat is permanently forfeited to the next eligible candidate.

Section 2.3: Course Withdrawal Procedures and Transcript Notations

1.  Following the conclusion of the Add/Drop Period, a student may elect to
    withdraw from a course without academic penalty by submitting an Electronic
    Course Withdrawal Form, endorsed by their Academic Advisor, up to the end of
    the ninth (9th) instructional week of the semester.
2.  A course withdrawal approved under this section shall result in an
    administrative notation of W on the student's official transcript. The W
    notation carries zero credit value, is excluded from grade point average
    computations, but counts as attempted credits for academic progression
    calculations.
3.  Withdrawals submitted after the ninth instructional week are prohibited, and
    the instructor must award a terminal letter grade (A through F) based upon
    the cumulative performance evaluations completed by the student over the
    entirety of the semester.
4.  A student is restricted to a maximum aggregate limit of four (4) W notations
    over the entire duration of an undergraduate degree program. Any subsequent
    request for withdrawal beyond this four-course cap will be automatically
    denied, obliging the student to finish the course and receive an earned
    letter grade.

Section 2.4: Credit Overloads, Underloads, and Concurrent Enrolment

1.  Any student wishing to register for fewer than twelve (12) credit units
    (Underload) must obtain written authorization from the Dean of their School,
    acknowledging that part-time status may adversely affect residential housing
    privileges, institutional financial aid, visa compliance for international
    students, and varsity athletic eligibility.
2.  Credit overloads exceeding twenty-one (21) credit units up to a maximum cap
    of twenty-four (24) credit units may be granted only to students who have
    attained a minimum Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of 3.75, have
    successfully cleared all prerequisite requirements, and possess no active
    disciplinary investigations.
3.  Concurrent enrollment in another degree, diploma, or certificate program at
    an external tertiary institution while actively matriculated at this
    University is strictly prohibited without the prior written concurrence of
    the Academic Registrar.

Section 2.5: Leaves of Absence, Interruption of Study, and Readmission

1.  A student in good academic standing (CGPA \ge 2.00) who has completed at
    least one full semester of study may petition for a formal Voluntary Leave
    of Absence (VLOA) for personal, educational, or professional reasons for an
    aggregate duration not exceeding two (2) consecutive semesters.
2.  Applications for a VLOA must be lodged with the Office of the Academic
    Registrar no later than fourteen (14) calendar days prior to the start of
    the semester for which the leave is sought.
3.  A student returning from an approved VLOA must submit a formal Intent to
    Return Notice at least thirty (30) days before the commencement of the
    targeted semester to facilitate registration access and catalog alignment.
4.  Failure to return upon the expiration of an authorized VLOA, or unexcused
    non-registration for an ongoing academic term, constitutes spontaneous
    abandonment of studies, resulting in administrative termination of
    matriculation. Students so terminated must undergo the full competitive
    readmission process under the prevailing academic catalog.

Section 2.6: Auditing of Modules and Non-Credit Participation

1.  Students in good academic standing may register to audit a maximum of one
    course per semester, subject to explicit written approval by the course
    instructor and the availability of physical seating capacity.
2.  Audited courses do not yield academic credits, do not contribute to degree
    requirements, and do not influence GPA calculations. The student's
    transcript will reflect an audit designation of AU provided the instructor
    confirms that the student met the agreed-upon attendance expectations.
3.  Auditors are exempt from submitting final examinations or graded terminal
    projects, but must satisfy all baseline decorum and basic participation
    rules established by the instructor.

Chapter 3: Attendance, Coursework, and Participation

Section 3.1: Minimum Attendance Requirement for Examination Eligibility

1.  Punctual and regular attendance at all scheduled lectures, laboratory
    sessions, seminars, tutorials, and practical workshops is a mandatory
    academic obligation for every registered student.
2.  All matriculated students must maintain an absolute minimum threshold of 75%
    attendance across lectures, practicals, and tutorials in each enrolled
    course to be deemed eligible to appear for semester-end examinations.
3.  This 75% requirement is mandatory, and no departmental authority,
    instructor, or dean possesses the jurisdiction to relax this threshold under
    any circumstances.
4.  An instructor shall compute attendance by dividing the total number of
    sessions attended by the total number of scheduled contact hours from the
    first instructional day to the final instructional day of the semester.
5.  Any student whose cumulative attendance falls below the mandatory 75%
    threshold at the conclusion of the instructional period shall be summarily
    debarred from sitting the final examination for that module.
6.  Debarment under this section shall result in the immediate and unalterable
    assignment of an F-NA (Failure due to Non-Attendance) grade on the student's
    official transcript, which carries 0.00 quality points and shall be
    permanently computed into the student’s Term Grade Point Average (TGPA) and
    Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA).

Section 3.2: Verification and Electronic Tracking of Attendance

1.  Attendance records must be systematically updated and published via the
    institutional Learning Management System (LMS) by the course instructor
    within forty-eight (48) hours of the conclusion of each scheduled class
    session.
2.  Where automated biometric, radio frequency identification (RFID) turnstiles,
    or Bluetooth-beacon check-in kiosks are deployed, students must
    independently execute their attendance scan upon physical entry into the
    lecture hall.
3.  Any attempt to record attendance for an absent peer, manipulate RFID
    signals, execute proxy check-ins, or alter electronic attendance logs
    represents a Tier-III disciplinary offense under the Student Code of
    Conduct, triggering immediate referral to the Senate Disciplinary Board.

Section 3.3: In-Class Decorum and Instructional Disruption

1.  Instructors maintain ultimate authority over the pedagogical environment of
    their classrooms, laboratories, and physical or virtual instructional
    spaces.
2.  Instructors may exclude from a class session any student whose behavior is
    disruptive, abusive, obstreperous, or persistently non-compliant with
    standard safety and pedagogical instructions.
3.  Any student formally expelled from a class session for behavioral disruption
    shall be marked absent for that contact hour, and such exclusion shall count
    directly against the student's overall attendance calculation under
    Section 3.1.
4.  Persistent removal from a class (occurring on three or more occasions during
    a single term) shall result in an administrative referral to the Dean of
    Students and the immediate initiation of formal suspension proceedings.

Section 3.4: Coursework Submission Deadlines and Grace Windows

1.  Coursework assignments, lab reports, reflective essays, term papers, and
    problem sets must be submitted electronically via the University LMS before
    the designated cut-off date and hour established by the course syllabus.
2.  Unless otherwise explicitly articulated in the course syllabus, a standard
    penalty of five percent (5%) of the total available assignment score shall
    be deducted for every twenty-four (24) hour period, or fraction thereof,
    that an assignment is submitted post-deadline.
3.  Coursework submitted more than seventy-two (72) hours following the original
    deadline shall receive an automatic numerical grade of zero (0), with no
    provision for retroactive grading, except in cases of catastrophic
    technological failure of university-controlled IT infrastructure verified by
    the Chief Information Officer.

Section 3.5: Field Trips, Co-Curricular Travel, and Class Absences

1.  Participation in authorized academic field trips, institutional delegations,
    cultural symposia, or student government conferences does not automatically
    exempt students from instructional responsibilities.
2.  Faculty sponsors of university-sanctioned travel must file an official
    Notification of Authorized Group Travel with the Office of the Academic
    Registrar at least fourteen (14) days prior to the proposed travel date.
3.  Students embarking on approved institutional travel remain personally
    responsible for coordinating with course instructors to make up missed
    laboratory exercises, quizzes, or project deliverables within seven (7) days
    of their return to campus.

Section 3.6: Continuous Assessment and Midterm Monitoring

1.  Continuous assessment components—including quizzes, homework portfolios,
    mid-semester evaluations, and practical laboratory tests—must comprise not
    less than 40% and not more than 60% of the aggregate grade in any standard
    lecture-based course.
2.  Instructors are mandated to publish progressive continuous assessment grades
    on the SIS by the end of the eighth (8th) instructional week, providing
    students with formal mid-semester performance transparency prior to the
    withdrawal deadline.

Chapter 4: Assessment, Grading Scale, and Academic Records

Section 4.1: Unified University Grading Scale

1.  Academic achievement in all undergraduate and taught postgraduate courses is
    evaluated on a ten-point (10.00) grading scale, mapped to standard
    alphabetical letter grades, percentage intervals, and academic performance
    descriptors as delineated in the following schedule:

| Letter Grade | Grade Point Value | Percentage Range | Descriptive Evaluation |
| :----------: | :---------------: | :--------------: | :--------------------- |
| A+           | 10.00             | 90% – 100%       | Outstanding            |
| A            | 9.00              | 80% – 89.99%     | Excellent              |
| B+           | 8.00              | 70% – 79.99%     | Very Good              |
| B            | 7.00              | 60% – 69.99%     | Good                   |
| C+           | 6.00              | 55% – 59.99%     | Satisfactory           |
| C            | 5.00              | 50% – 54.99%     | Pass                   |
| D            | 4.00              | 40% – 49.99%     | Marginal Pass          |
| F            | 0.00              | Below 40%        | Fail                   |
| F-NA         | 0.00              | Debarred         | Non-Attendance Failure |

2.  Passing grades for all undergraduate degree requirements are A+, A, B+, B,
    C+, C, and D. Postgraduate degree programs require a minimum grade of C
    (5.00 grade points) to satisfy program-specific core curriculum
    requirements.
3.  An earned grade of F or F-NA represents failure, confers zero credit units
    toward graduation, and is computed directly into the student’s Term Grade
    Point Average (TGPA) and Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA).

Section 4.2: Computation of Grade Point Averages (TGPA and CGPA)

1.  The Term Grade Point Average (TGPA) is computed at the termination of each
    academic semester by dividing the sum of the quality points earned (the
    product of the credit units assigned to a module and the numeric grade point
    value earned) by the total number of credit units attempted in that term:
    \text{TGPA} = \frac{\sum (\text{Credit Units}_i \times \text{Grade Points}_i)}{\sum \text{Credit Units}_i}
2.  The Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) is computed across all
    instructional terms completed at the University, incorporating all attempted
    courses that carry academic credit units, using the same mathematical ratio
    across the cumulative aggregate of attempted credits.
3.  When an undergraduate student repeats an eligible course under Section 4.5,
    both grades remain on the permanent transcript, but only the higher grade
    point value earned shall be incorporated into the calculation of the CGPA.

Section 4.3: Grade Re-evaluation, Scrutiny, and Discrepancy Deadlines

1.  The evaluation of final examination answer scripts, major term
    dissertations, and capstone assessments is conducted under strict
    professional and institutional standards.
2.  A student who identifies an alleged substantive error in the grading of
    their final examination script must submit a formal Grade Re-evaluation
    Application to the Office of the Controller of Examinations within exactly
    seven (7) calendar days of the official publication of semester results.
3.  Applications submitted after this strict 7-calendar-day window will be
    summarily rejected, and published grades shall be considered permanently
    final and unalterable.
4.  Each re-evaluation application must be accompanied by an administrative
    filing fee of $50 per course and an itemized justification citing specific
    errors in marking or calculation; general expressions of dissatisfaction
    with an awarded mark do not constitute valid grounds for re-evaluation.
5.  If a re-evaluation results in a variance of more than five percent (5%) from
    the original mark, the script shall be evaluated by an external third-party
    examiner appointed by the Dean, whose grade shall be final, and the filing
    fee shall be fully refunded to the student.

Section 4.4: Incomplete Grades and Administrative Notations

1.  An administrative notation of I (Incomplete) may be temporarily assigned by
    an instructor only when an extenuating, documented emergency (such as
    severe, sudden illness or bereavement) prevents a student in good standing
    from finishing a discrete, final component of a course (e.g., the final
    paper or final exam).
2.  To qualify for an I notation, the student must have satisfactorily completed
    at least 70% of the total coursework with a passing average prior to the
    onset of the emergency.
3.  All incomplete coursework must be submitted, graded, and certified no later
    than four (4) weeks following the first instructional day of the subsequent
    semester. If the outstanding work is not fully reconciled within this 4-week
    window, the I notation automatically converts to an earned failing grade of
    F on the official transcript.

Section 4.5: Course Repetition and Grade Replacement Limits

1.  An undergraduate student who receives a final grade of D, F, or F-NA in any
    course may repeat that course for grade replacement purposes.
2.  A student may repeat a maximum of four (4) distinct courses during their
    entire undergraduate tenure. No single course may be repeated more than
    twice (i.e., a maximum of three total attempts).
3.  All attempts remain an indelible part of the student’s historical
    transcript, but only the highest achieved grade point shall be utilized in
    the calculation of the final CGPA.

Section 4.6: Dean’s List and Academic Honors Distinctions

1.  Following the conclusion of each regular Fall and Spring semester, the
    Office of the Academic Registrar shall publish the Dean’s Honor List.
2.  To qualify for the Dean’s List, an undergraduate student must have completed
    a minimum of fifteen (15) graded credit units during the semester, earned a
    TGPA of not less than 8.75, have no incomplete grades (I), and possess no
    findings of academic or non-academic disciplinary culpability on record.
3.  Baccalaureate degrees are conferred with permanent Latin honors based upon
    final graduating CGPA:
      - Cum Laude: CGPA of 8.50 to 8.99
      - Magna Cum Laude: CGPA of 9.00 to 9.49
      - Summa Cum Laude: CGPA of 9.50 to 10.00

Chapter 5: Medical Leave, Compassionate Exceptions, and Special Accommodations

Section 5.1: University Health Services Mandate and Medical Protocols

1.  The University Health Service (UHS), under the clinical direction of the
    Chief Medical Officer (CMO), is the sole institutional authority responsible
    for evaluating, validating, and endorsing all medical certifications,
    clinical excuses, and health-related leave petitions.
2.  Any illness, psychiatric crisis, surgical operation, or severe physical
    incapacitation that impedes a student's ability to attend classes or
    complete scheduled academic assessments must be reported to UHS immediately.
3.  Retrospective medical certificates issued by external private medical
    practitioners without contemporaneous emergency room or hospital admission
    records shall not be recognized or accepted by UHS or the Registrar.

Section 5.2: Submission Timelines for Medical Documentation

1.  Students seeking official medical endorsement for any missed instructional
    session, midterm evaluation, or assignment deadline must submit their
    medical certificates to UHS within forty-eight (48) hours of returning to
    active campus presence.
2.  Documentation must include a full clinical summary from a certified
    physician, diagnostic laboratory findings where applicable, prescription
    receipts, and an official clinical evaluation detailing the exact duration
    of physical incapacitation.
3.  UHS reserves the unreserved legal right to contact treating physicians,
    examine hospital admission databases, or convene an independent clinical
    board to review the veracity of any submitted medical proof.

Section 5.3: Medical Withdrawal from Entire Semesters

1.  If a student experiences an acute, prolonged, or catastrophic medical
    illness that precludes academic progress for more than four (4) consecutive
    weeks, they may petition for a Full Medical Withdrawal (FMW) from the active
    semester.
2.  A petition for an FMW must be endorsed by the Chief Medical Officer and
    approved by the Dean of the student's Faculty.
3.  Upon approval of an FMW, all courses enrolled in during that semester shall
    receive a permanent transcript notation of WM (Medical Withdrawal). WM
    notations do not affect GPA calculations and are exempt from the standard
    four-course withdrawal cap defined in Section 2.3.

Section 5.4: Medical Concessions and Severe Health Waivers for Course Attendance

1.  The University recognizes that severe, unforeseen medical conditions can
    fundamentally disrupt a student’s physical capacity to attend scheduled
    classes.
2.  In the event of documented hospitalization, prolonged debilitating illness,
    or major surgical intervention, a student who submits certified medical
    documentation via the University Health Services within five (5) business
    days of discharge may have their minimum attendance threshold lowered
    to 50%.
3.  Upon verification by the Chief Medical Officer, the student shall
    automatically be granted full examination clearance provided their verified
    attendance is at or above 50%.
4.  Course instructors shall be officially notified by the Office of the
    Academic Registrar of the revised 50% medical threshold and are mandated to
    permit the student to sit for all final examination assessments without
    grade deduction, penalty, or debarment.
5.  In circumstances where the student's verified attendance remains below 50%
    despite certified medical hospitalization, the student cannot receive an
    attendance waiver and must be processed for a Full Medical Withdrawal under
    Section 5.3.

Section 5.5: Compassionate Leave for Family Bereavement

1.  A student may request an excused Compassionate Leave of Absence for a period
    not exceeding five (5) consecutive business days in the tragic event of the
    death of an immediate family member.
2.  For the purposes of this section, an "immediate family member" is strictly
    defined as a parent, legal guardian, sibling, spouse, or biological/adopted
    child.
3.  Official verification—consisting of a certified death certificate, obituary
    notice, or funeral director's statement—must be filed with the Office of the
    Dean of Students within ten (10) calendar days of returning to campus to
    excuse instructional absences accrued during the leave.

Section 5.6: Disability Accommodations and Accessibility Services

1.  Students with permanent or temporary physical, sensory, cognitive, or
    psychiatric disabilities are entitled to reasonable academic accommodations
    through the Office of Student Accessibility Services (SAS).
2.  Accommodations may include, but are not limited to, extended time on
    examinations (up to a standard 50% addition), quiet examination
    environments, assistive screen-reading software, physical note-takers, or
    specialized laboratory ergonomic furniture.
3.  Retroactive accommodation requests for assessments, tests, or examinations
    already administered and graded are strictly inadmissible and will not be
    considered under any circumstances.

Chapter 6: Examination Administration, Proctoring, and Integrity

Section 6.1: Examination Schedules, Seating Plans, and Candidate Conduct

1.  Final examination timetables are prepared and promulgated by the Office of
    the Controller of Examinations at least four (4) weeks prior to the
    commencement of the official examination period.
2.  Students must be present at the assigned examination venue no later than
    twenty (20) minutes prior to the scheduled commencement of the test. No
    student will be admitted to the examination room after thirty (30) minutes
    have elapsed from the start of the paper.
3.  Students must display their valid physical University Student Identification
    Card upon their assigned examination desk for the entire duration of the
    test. Candidates lacking valid photographic identification will be denied
    entry.
4.  No candidate may permanently or temporarily vacate the examination room
    during the first forty-five (45) minutes or during the final fifteen (15)
    minutes of the scheduled examination session.

Section 6.2: Permitted and Prohibited Materials in Examination Venues

1.  Unless explicitly designated as an "Open-Book Examination" by the course
    instructor on the official test paper header, all examinations are strictly
    closed-book.
2.  Candidates are strictly prohibited from bringing into the examination hall
    any electronic communication devices, cellular telephones, programmable
    calculators, smart watches, wearable recording gear, digital audio players,
    notes, unauthorized scratch paper, or bags.
3.  All unauthorized materials must be deposited in designated storage cubicles
    outside the examination hall at the sole risk of the candidate; the
    University disclaims all liability for the loss, theft, or damage of
    personal property deposited in such storage areas.
4.  The possession of an unauthorized electronic device or physical crib sheet
    within the examination hall, irrespective of whether the candidate attempted
    to access or use the device or material, constitutes an immediate,
    irrebuttable presumption of academic dishonesty.

Section 6.3: Invigilation Protocols, Proctor Authority, and Incident Reporting

1.  Examination invigilators (proctors) possess full plenary authority to
    preserve order, enforce examination security protocols, inspect materials,
    and direct the seating or relocation of any candidate.
2.  If an invigilator reasonably suspects a candidate of cheating, unauthorized
    communication, plagiarism, or copying from another student, the invigilator
    shall immediately confiscate all relevant physical evidence, endorse the
    candidate's answer script with an explanatory note and timestamp, and
    provide the candidate with a fresh answer script to complete the remaining
    duration of the test.
3.  The candidate shall not be physically ejected from the examination room
    unless their conduct is actively violent, disruptive, or obstreperous toward
    invigilators or fellow candidates.
4.  Within three (3) hours of the conclusion of the examination, the lead
    invigilator must submit a formal Comprehensive Incident Report, together
    with all confiscated materials, to the Controller of Examinations and the
    Senate Committee on Academic Integrity.

Section 6.4: Examination Clashes and Rescheduling Directives

1.  An "Examination Clash" is defined strictly as:
      - Two examinations scheduled concurrently at the exact same hour and date;
        or
      - Three examinations scheduled on the same calendar date spanning
        consecutive time slots.
2.  Having two examinations on the same day with an intervening break, or having
    examinations on consecutive calendar days, does not constitute an
    examination clash.
3.  Students affected by a certified clash must submit an Examination Clash
    Rescheduling Form to the Office of the Controller of Examinations no later
    than fourteen (14) days prior to the start of the examination period. The
    Controller shall schedule a supervised alternative sitting during the
    reserve examination window.

Section 6.5: Take-Home, Online, and Remote Examination Rules

1.  Take-home examinations must be completed exclusively by the enrolled
    individual without collaboration, consultation, or outside assistance from
    peers, tutors, or artificial intelligence systems, unless collaborative work
    is explicitly permitted in writing on the examination rubric.
2.  Remote proctored examinations require the continuous operation of both an
    active visual camera and a functional microphone throughout the duration of
    the testing period.
3.  System interruptions, camera disconnections, or unexplained network outages
    exceeding five (5) cumulative minutes during a remote online examination
    shall result in the immediate voiding of the testing attempt, pending formal
    administrative investigation.

Section 6.6: Retention of Examination Scripts and Archival Protocols

1.  Graded final examination answer scripts, electronic exam submissions,
    continuous evaluation portfolios, and official grade allocation sheets must
    be preserved securely by the academic departments for a minimum period of
    three (3) full calendar years from the date of grade finalization.
2.  Following the expiration of the statutory three-year retention period,
    physical examination scripts may be scheduled for destruction via
    confidential industrial shredding under the supervision of the University
    Records Manager.

Chapter 7: Financial Aid, Fee Structures, and Institutional Scholarships

Section 7.1: Tuition Billing Schedules, Payment Deadlines, and Late Charges

1.  Tuition fees, laboratory charges, library levies, residential housing room
    costs, and associated administrative dues are billed on a semester basis via
    the University Financial Portal.
2.  Full settlement of all assessed charges, or the successful enrollment in an
    approved Institutional Tuition Deferred Payment Plan, must be executed no
    later than 17:00 on the Friday preceding the first day of instructional
    classes.
3.  Unpaid balances remaining after the designated deadline will incur an
    initial Late Payment Surcharge of $150, plus a recurring monthly compounding
    interest penalty of 1.5% on the outstanding unpaid balance.
4.  Accounts remaining delinquent upon the completion of the fourth week of
    classes will trigger an immediate administrative Financial Hold, blocking
    access to LMS course content, library borrowing privileges, official
    transcript generation, and future semester registration.

Section 7.2: Institutional Merit Scholarship Criteria and Renewal Standards

1.  Institutional Merit Scholarships (Category A and Category B) are awarded to
    recruit and sustain candidates exhibiting outstanding academic distinction.
2.  Institutional Merit Scholarships (Category A and Category B) require
    recipients to maintain an unbroken Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of
    not less than 8.50 on a 10.00 scale at the conclusion of every academic
    year, with zero backlogs, grade penalties, or repeat modules.
3.  Failure to achieve an 8.50 CGPA or the accumulation of any backlog in any
    semester results in the irrevocable termination of the scholarship award
    without exception across all academic and co-curricular cohorts.
4.  Students whose merit scholarships are revoked under this clause cannot
    petition for retroactive reinstatement, nor may they offset a CGPA deficit
    through summer remedial coursework or grade replacements.
5.  All merit scholarship recipients are obligated to enroll in a full course
    load (minimum of fifteen credit units per semester) and must demonstrate
    exemplary character, free of any disciplinary warnings or integrity
    citations.

Section 7.3: Need-Based Grants, Bursaries, and Emergency Assistance

1.  The University maintains a Dedicated Student Need-Based Bursary Fund to
    assist economically disadvantaged matriculated students who demonstrate
    acute financial distress.
2.  Applicants for need-based grants must submit comprehensive independent
    financial disclosures, including national tax declarations, verified family
    income statements, asset declarations, and bank records for all residing
    household members.
3.  Bursary awards are evaluated annually by the Financial Aid Committee. If an
    applicant intentionally conceals financial resources, reports fraudulent
    income metrics, or fails to disclose third-party sponsorships, all disbursed
    funds shall become immediately repayable, and the student shall face
    expulsion.

Section 7.4: Tuition Refund Policies for Withdrawals and Cancellations

1.  A student who executes a formal Voluntary University Withdrawal from the
    institution must submit an official University Clearance Form to the
    Registrar to initiate a financial refund.
2.  Tuition fee refunds are calculated strictly in accordance with the official
    refund matrix below, calculated from the first official day of the semester:
      - Withdrawal prior to Day 1 of classes: 100% tuition refund (less
        non-refundable registration deposit)
      - Withdrawal during Weeks 1 and 2 (Add/Drop Period): 80% tuition refund
      - Withdrawal during Weeks 3 and 4: 50% tuition refund
      - Withdrawal during Weeks 5 through 8: 20% tuition refund
      - Withdrawal after Week 8: 0% tuition refund (100% financial liability)
3.  Non-tuition fees, including technology levies, health service fees, student
    activity dues, and laboratory breakage deposits, are wholly non-refundable
    after the first day of classes.

Section 7.5: Work-Study Employment and On-Campus Student Labor

1.  Matriculated full-time undergraduate students are eligible to apply for
    on-campus employment positions under the Federal and Institutional
    Work-Study Programs.
2.  Students employed in work-study capacities may not work in excess of twenty
    (20) hours per week during active instructional semesters, or in excess of
    forty (40) hours per week during official vacation recesses.
3.  A student-employee whose CGPA falls below 2.50 shall have their work-study
    eligibility suspended immediately until satisfactory academic progress is
    re-established.

Section 7.6: External Scholarships and Third-Party Sponsorships

1.  Students receiving scholarships, fellowships, or educational subsidies from
    external foundations, corporations, civic organizations, or foreign
    governments must formally declare such awards to the Office of Financial Aid
    within fourteen (14) days of award confirmation.
2.  The University reserves the right to recalculate and adjust institutional
    need-based aid packages to ensure that total financial assistance does not
    exceed the aggregate official Cost of Attendance (COA) established by the
    Board of Regents.

Chapter 8: Codes of Student Conduct, Integrity, and Disciplinary Procedures

Section 8.1: Academic Integrity and Categories of Misconduct

1.  Academic integrity is fundamental to the scholarly mission of the
    University. The following non-exhaustive acts constitute severe violations
    of academic integrity:
      - Plagiarism: Submitting, copying, incorporating, or paraphrasing another
        individual's intellectual labor, words, ideas, data, or code without
        full, standard, and explicit academic citation.
      - Fabrication and Falsification: Inventing, distorting, forging, or
        manipulating research data, clinical observations, survey results,
        laboratory metrics, or bibliography entries.
      - Unauthorized Collaboration: Working collaboratively on assignments,
        projects, or assessments specifically designated by the instructor as
        individual work.
      - Contract Cheating and Impersonation: Paying, commissioning, or arranging
        for a commercial entity, external individual, or peer to complete
        coursework or examinations, or impersonating another student during an
        assessment.
      - Unauthorized AI Utilization: Generating, altering, or translating graded
        academic submissions through generative artificial intelligence models
        or algorithmic synthesis tools, unless explicitly authorized in writing
        by the instructor's syllabus.

Section 8.2: Disciplinary Sanctions for Integrity Violations

1.  Suspected violations of academic integrity shall be investigated by the
    Senate Committee on Academic Integrity in accordance with due process.
2.  Upon a finding of culpability, the Committee is empowered to administer one
    or more of the following formal disciplinary penalties:
      - Tier I: Written warning, formal letter of reprimand placed in the
        internal file, and an unalterable grade of zero (0) on the disputed
        assignment or quiz.
      - Tier II: An irreversible assignment of an F grade in the course,
        forfeiture of honors eligibility, and cancellation of any institutional
        leadership positions.
      - Tier III: Mandatory suspension from the University for a duration
        ranging from one (1) to four (4) full academic semesters.
      - Tier IV: Permanent, irreversible Expulsion from the University, with an
        indelible notation of "Expelled for Academic Misconduct" recorded on the
        official transcript.

Section 8.3: Non-Academic Misconduct and General Campus Discipline

1.  Students must conduct themselves at all times in a manner consistent with
    civil order, safety, mutual respect, and the rule of law.
2.  The following behaviors constitute severe violations of the Non-Academic
    Code of Conduct:
      - Physical assault, battery, harassment, stalking, bullying, intimidation,
        or physical threats directed toward any student, faculty member, staff,
        or visitor.
      - Unlawful manufacture, distribution, sale, possession, or consumption of
        controlled narcotics, illicit drugs, or unauthorized prescription
        medications on campus grounds.
      - Hazing, defined as any forced, coerced, or degrading activity designed
        to humiliate, endanger, or physically harm an individual as a condition
        of initiation into an organization, athletic team, or
        fraternity/sorority.
      - Intentional damage, vandalism, theft, arson, or unauthorized entry into
        University buildings, computational facilities, or restricted grounds.

Section 8.4: Disciplinary Investigation, Due Process, and Tribunal Hearings

1.  All non-academic disciplinary complaints must be submitted in writing to the
    Office of the Dean of Students.
2.  If the Dean determines that prima facie evidence supports the allegations,
    the case shall be referred to the University Disciplinary Tribunal.
3.  The accused student shall receive formal written notice of charges, an
    itemized summary of evidence, and at least seven (7) business days to
    prepare their defense.
4.  The student is entitled to appear in person before the Tribunal, present
    relevant witnesses, inspect all inculpatory evidence, and be accompanied by
    a silent advisor of their choosing (who may be legal counsel, provided
    forty-eight hours' advance notice is given).

Section 8.5: Disciplinary Sanction Hierarchy and Permanent Records

1.  The University Disciplinary Tribunal may issue sanctions including formal
    warnings, community restitution service, monetary fines, eviction from
    residential facilities, disciplinary probation, suspension, or permanent
    expulsion.
2.  Disciplinary probations and suspensions remain recorded in the student's
    central administrative record and shall be disclosed to external licensing
    bodies, graduate admissions boards, or employers when formal inquiries
    regarding disciplinary standing are submitted with a signed release.

Section 8.6: Alcohol, Substance, and Weapons Prohibitions

1.  The University campus is an absolute weapons-free zone. The possession,
    storage, display, or discharge of firearms, explosive materials, hazardous
    chemical devices, martial arts weapons, or knives with blades exceeding
    three inches is strictly prohibited, even where individuals hold external
    civilian permits.
2.  The University campus, academic quadrangles, recreational grounds, and
    residential housing are alcohol-free spaces for all individuals under the
    legal drinking age; unauthorized alcohol distribution or public intoxication
    is subject to immediate disciplinary referral.

Chapter 9: Extracurricular Governance, Student Organizations, and Athletics

Section 9.1: Varsity Athlete Standing, Athletic Concessions, and Scholarship Retention

1.  Student-athletes officially rostered on university varsity sports teams
    representing the institution in sanctioned intercollegiate competitions are
    entitled to retain their institutional merit scholarships and athletic grant
    allocations provided they maintain a minimum CGPA of 8.00 on a 10.00 scale
    and complete at least 24 academic units per year.
2.  Varsity athletes falling within the CGPA range of 8.00 to 8.49 remain
    eligible for full scholarship disbursements, competition travel
    authorizations, and department-funded academic tutoring support.
3.  The Department of Intercollegiate Athletics shall conduct end-of-semester
    grade audits to ensure varsity athletes comply with all applicable athletic
    conference standing criteria and institutional benchmarks.

Section 9.2: Recognition and Chartering of Student Organizations

1.  All student clubs, cultural societies, academic associations, and interest
    groups must obtain a formal annual charter from the Student Government
    Association (SGA) and the Office of Student Activities.
2.  To qualify for a formal charter, an organization must demonstrate a
    membership roster of not less than fifteen (15) matriculated students,
    possess an endorsed constitution, and secure a full-time university faculty
    or staff member to serve as official Faculty Advisor.
3.  Student organizations are strictly prohibited from maintaining
    discrimination in their membership criteria based on race, nationality,
    religious affiliation, gender identity, sexual orientation, or disability
    status.

Section 9.3: Financial Management and Funding Allocations for Clubs

1.  All chartered student organizations must maintain their financial treasury
    balances within the official University Financial Accounting System.
2.  Off-campus commercial bank accounts opened under the name of a student club,
    society, or athletic team are strictly prohibited and represent grounds for
    immediate charter revocation and asset freezing.
3.  Financial disbursements from club accounts require the dual signatures of
    the student organization Treasurer and the designated Faculty Advisor, along
    with final audit clearance by the Office of Student Activities.

Section 9.4: Travel Authorization and Field Representation for Student Groups

1.  Any off-campus excursion, competition tour, debate tournament, or service
    mission involving student organizations requires an approved Off-Campus
    Travel Application filed thirty (30) days in advance.
2.  Every participating student must execute an official Liability Waiver and
    Emergency Medical Information Release Form prior to departure.
3.  The University disclaims all legal and financial liability for unsanctioned
    trips, rogue tours, or private student travel undertaken without the formal
    written authorization of the Dean of Students.

Section 9.5: Campus Demonstrations, Assemblies, and Free Speech Clearances

1.  The University affirms the fundamental rights of free inquiry, robust
    discourse, and peaceful assembly.
2.  To ensure that expressive assemblies do not disrupt direct instructional
    delivery, block egress routes, or compromise physical security, student
    organizers must submit a Public Assembly Notification Form at least
    seventy-two (72) hours prior to the planned demonstration.
3.  Assemblies are restricted to designated Free Expression Zones, and the
    utilization of artificial sound amplification systems (megaphones, PA
    speakers) is strictly prohibited within one hundred (100) yards of any
    classroom facility, library, or administrative building during standard
    instructional hours.

Section 9.6: Use of University Name, Emblems, and Intellectual Property

1.  The University name, crest, seal, mascot, athletic logos, and official
    wordmarks are the exclusive intellectual property of the University.
2.  No student, club, organization, or external commercial partner may utilize
    university trademarks, emblazon clothing, produce marketing merchandise, or
    operate digital platforms using the institutional name without explicit
    written licensing permission from the Office of University Communications.

Chapter 10: Residential Life, On-Campus Housing, and Dining

Section 10.1: Eligibility, Housing Agreements, and Occupancy Rules

1.  On-campus residential housing is an educational privilege accorded to
    matriculated full-time undergraduate and postgraduate students in good
    academic and disciplinary standing.
2.  Every residential student must execute an annual Housing Occupancy Contract,
    which establishes the legal conditions, behavioral expectations, and
    financial liabilities associated with university room tenancies.
3.  Housing agreements are non-transferable. Subletting, transferring, renting,
    or assigning an assigned dorm room, suite, or bed to another
    individual—whether a matriculated student or a third party—is strictly
    prohibited and constitutes grounds for immediate eviction and a
    non-negotiable $1,000 fine.

Section 10.2: Room Inspections, Cleanliness, and Maintenance Access

1.  The University Housing Division reserves the unreserved right to conduct
    routine, scheduled health and safety inspections of all residential suites,
    dorm rooms, and common quarters upon giving twenty-four (24) hours of
    electronic notice.
2.  Authorized university maintenance personnel, facilities contractors, and
    housing administrators may enter any residential room without prior notice
    in the event of an imminent physical emergency, fire alarm, utility failure,
    or reasonable suspicion of immediate danger to life, health, or property.
3.  Residents are strictly required to maintain their assigned rooms in a
    sanitary, clean, and fire-safe condition, free of accumulated refuse,
    hazardous materials, or blocked doorways.

Section 10.3: Quiet Hours, Courtesy Hours, and Noise Regulations

1.  To foster an environment conducive to intensive scholarly inquiry and
    adequate rest, mandatory "Quiet Hours" are enforced in all university
    residential facilities during the following times:
      - Sunday through Thursday: 22:00 to 08:00
      - Friday and Saturday: 23:59 to 09:00
2.  During designated Quiet Hours, sound emissions emanating from stereos,
    televisions, musical instruments, conversations, or parties must not be
    audible beyond the closed boundaries of a resident's room or suite.
3.  A continuous twenty-four-hour (24/7) Quiet Hours regimen automatically
    enters into force across all residential halls starting at 00:01 on the
    first day of the formal Final Examination Period and persists continuously
    until the end of the term.

Section 10.4: Guest Policies, Overnight Visitation, and Entry Security

1.  Non-resident visitors and guests are permitted in university residence halls
    only during designated visiting hours (09:00 to 22:00 daily).
2.  All non-resident guests must physically register their entry at the
    Residence Hall Security Reception Desk by presenting valid,
    government-issued photo identification, and they must be accompanied by
    their resident host at all times.
3.  Overnight stays by any guest are strictly capped at a maximum of two (2)
    consecutive nights, and under no circumstances may a guest remain in a
    residence hall for more than four (4) aggregate nights within any thirty-day
    calendar period.
4.  Cohabitational arrangements with non-students or unauthorized peers are
    categorically prohibited.

Section 10.5: Fire Safety Standards, Prohibited Appliances, and Cooking

1.  Compliance with fire and life safety codes is an absolute requirement.
    Tampering with smoke detectors, hanging items from fire sprinkler heads,
    discharging fire extinguishers without cause, or covering alarm strobes is a
    Tier-II disciplinary offense resulting in immediate housing probation and
    mandatory restitution costs.
2.  The possession, operation, or storage of high-draw electrical heating
    appliances—including portable space heaters, hot plates, toaster ovens,
    electric fryers, open-coil cooking units, and candle-warmers—is strictly
    banned inside student dormitory rooms.
3.  Cooking is permitted exclusively in designated communal kitchen galleys
    equipped with automated commercial fire suppression infrastructure.

Section 10.6: Dining Plans, Meal Swipes, and Dietary Operations

1.  All undergraduate students residing in conventional campus residence halls
    are mandated to purchase a comprehensive University Board (Dining) Plan for
    the entire duration of their housing tenure.
2.  Meal swipes, dining points, and meal vouchers are personal,
    non-transferable, and non-refundable. Swipes expire at the conclusion of
    each respective semester and do not roll over into subsequent instructional
    terms.
3.  The University Dining Service provides specialized dietary accommodations
    for students with documented clinical food allergies, celiac disease, or
    religious dietary requirements upon registration with the Dining Services
    Nutritionist.

Chapter 11: Academic Appeals, Re-checking, and Grievance Redressal

Section 11.1: Academic Re-checking, Transcript Audit, and Review Submissions

1.  The University guarantees the fundamental right of every matriculated
    student to request an administrative re-checking and mathematical audit of
    any final course grade, semester assessment, or transcript notation where
    clerical errors, tabulating omissions, or administrative anomalies are
    suspected.
2.  Any student seeking formal re-checking, clerical recount, or academic
    re-assessment of an evaluated final course component or transcript entry may
    submit an Academic Re-checking Petition to the Academic Ombudsperson within
    fourteen (14) working days from the date of official grade disclosure.
3.  Upon formal receipt of the petition, the Ombudsperson shall requisition all
    physical and digital examination scripts, score rubrics, and grade books
    from the relevant academic department.
4.  The designated academic review panel shall conduct a comprehensive
    re-checking to ensure that every question was fully marked, continuous
    assessment scores were correctly compiled, and transcript entries match
    verified department grade distributions.
5.  If an arithmetical or clerical error is confirmed during this 14-working-day
    review process, the Ombudsperson shall issue an administrative Grade
    Correction Directive, instructing the Office of the Academic Registrar to
    adjust the student's official records accordingly.

Section 11.2: Grounds for Formal Academic Appeals

1.  Substantive academic appeals disputing academic dismissals, course failures,
    academic dishonesty findings, or academic status changes are strictly
    restricted to the following actionable grounds:
      - Demonstrable procedural irregularity or substantial departure from
        established university policy during the assessment or administrative
        process;
      - Evident personal bias, prejudice, or documented animus on the part of an
        instructor or committee member;
      - Newly discovered substantive evidence that was genuinely unavailable at
        the time of the original decision and could not have been discovered
        through reasonable diligence.
2.  Disagreement with the academic judgment, intellectual grading standards, or
    rigor of an instructor shall never constitute legitimate grounds for appeal.

Section 11.3: Composition and Jurisdiction of the Academic Appeals Board

1.  The Academic Appeals Board (AAB) is an independent standing regulatory
    committee appointed by the University Senate, comprising three senior
    tenured faculty members, one representative from the Office of the
    Registrar, and one non-voting student ombudsperson.
2.  The AAB possesses sole jurisdiction to adjudicate formal appeals arising
    from academic status determinations, dismissals, and high-level disciplinary
    sanctions.
3.  The AAB is empowered to uphold original findings, reverse decisions, reduce
    imposed penalties, or remand cases back to departmental committees for a
    complete rehearing.

Section 11.4: Due Process, Evidentiary Standards, and Hearing Procedures

1.  In all proceedings before the AAB, the standard of proof applied shall be
    the "preponderance of the evidence" (i.e., whether it is more likely than
    not that the disputed circumstance or error occurred).
2.  The appellant student possesses the right to inspect all documentation
    submitted to the Board, submit written affidavits, call material witnesses,
    and present an oral summary of their grievance.
3.  All hearing proceedings of the AAB are confidential and closed to the
    public; verbatim stenographic or digital audio records must be securely
    archived for seven (7) years by the Office of the Registrar.

Section 11.5: Finality of Appellate Decisions and Presidential Review

1.  A written decision rendered by the Academic Appeals Board shall be delivered
    to the student, the Department Chair, and the Faculty Dean within ten (10)
    business days of the hearing's conclusion.
2.  The decision of the AAB is final, conclusive, and binding across all
    operational sectors of the University, subject only to an extraordinary
    petition for Executive Clemency or Presidential Review addressed to the
    University President within five (5) days of the ruling.
3.  Presidential Review is strictly limited to reviewing alleged constitutional
    violations or gross procedural malfeasance; the President shall not
    re-examine substantive academic judgments or alter factual findings.

Section 11.6: Protection Against Retaliation

1.  Any form of harassment, intimidation, grading retaliation, or professional
    disadvantage directed against a student who submits a good-faith academic
    appeal, grievance, or disciplinary complaint is strictly prohibited.
2.  Any faculty member, teaching assistant, or administrative employee found to
    have engaged in retaliatory behavior toward an appellant student shall face
    immediate disciplinary censure, suspension, or termination of employment
    under the University Faculty Handbook.

Chapter 12: Academic Discretion, Committee Petitions, and Extraordinary Waivers

Section 12.1: Authority and Scope of the Academic Committee

1.  The University Academic Committee (UAC) is the supreme executive operating
    body charged with supervising curriculum implementation, academic standards,
    graduation clearances, and the institutional application of Senate policies.
2.  The UAC convenes regularly to evaluate special petitions, interdisciplinary
    credit transfers, extraordinary academic waivers, and complex student
    petitions that transcend standard departmental boundaries.
3.  All discretionary powers exercised by the UAC must be executed within the
    boundaries of statutory governance, institutional integrity, and accredited
    pedagogical frameworks.

Section 12.2: Appeals for Extraordinary Attendance Condonement

1.  The University maintains that instructional presence and classroom
    engagement are paramount components of academic rigor and degree
    verification.
2.  Notwithstanding any other provisions in this rulebook or medical
    regulations, the University Academic Committee retains the sole and
    exclusive legal authority to evaluate petitions for attendance shortfalls.
3.  The Academic Committee may, upon formal review of extraordinary non-academic
    or compassionate hardship, waive the attendance requirement down to a
    minimum of 60%, and under no statutory provision or administrative power
    shall any waiver below 60% be approved or recognized by the university.
4.  Petitions under this section must be filed directly with the UAC Secretariat
    accompanied by corroborating independent evidence, written explanations of
    the extenuating hardship, and a formal character evaluation from the
    student's Faculty Dean.
5.  In the event that a student's attendance is condoned down to the
    authorized 60% threshold by the UAC, the student shall be permitted to sit
    for the final examination, but the maximum grade that may be awarded for the
    course shall be capped at a B (7.00 grade points).

Section 12.3: Credit Transfers and Course Substitutions

1.  A student seeking credit transfer for coursework completed at an external,
    regionally accredited tertiary institution must submit an official syllabus,
    reading list, and certified transcript to the Academic Committee.
2.  External transfer credits shall be granted only for courses wherein the
    student earned a grade equivalent to a B (3.00 on a 4.00 scale or 7.00 on
    a 10.00 scale) or higher.
3.  A maximum of sixty (60) credit units may be transferred toward an
    undergraduate baccalaureate degree; under no circumstances may institutional
    residency requirements be waived below the minimum requirement of sixty (60)
    credits earned directly in residence at this University.

Section 12.4: Graduation Clearances and Degree Audit Protocols

1.  The Office of the Academic Registrar executes an automated Degree Audit for
    every candidate nearing their scheduled completion semester to verify that
    all general education, core curriculum, major electives, and GPA criteria
    are fulfilled.
2.  A student must achieve an overall graduating CGPA of not less than 2.00
    across all attempted undergraduate coursework to be formally cleared for
    degree conferral.
3.  Candidates who maintain an unfulfilled financial obligation, library fine,
    housing fee, or unreturned university laboratory equipment shall have their
    diplomas, graduation regalia, and official transcripts withheld until
    complete financial clearance is established.

Section 12.5: Retroactive Degree Revocation

1.  The Board of Regents, acting upon the unanimous recommendation of the
    University Senate and the Academic Committee, possesses the plenary legal
    right to revoke any conferred degree, diploma, or certificate.
2.  Grounds for retroactive degree revocation include the posthumous or late
    discovery of academic fraud, extensive thesis plagiarism, experimental
    fabrication, criminal credential manipulation, or severe honor code
    transgressions committed during matriculation that went undetected prior to
    graduation.
3.  An individual whose degree is revoked shall be permanently expunged from the
    registry of university alumni, and formal notification shall be transmitted
    to professional licensing boards and national educational registries.

Section 12.6: Emergency Powers and Institutional Force Majeure

1.  In the catastrophic event of war, epidemics, natural disasters, national
    civic emergencies, or acts of God (Force Majeure) that physically disrupt
    the regular operation of the University, the Academic Committee, in
    concurrence with the President, is empowered to declare an Academic
    Emergency.
2.  During an authorized Academic Emergency, the Committee may temporarily
    institute alternative instructional delivery modes, modify grading rubrics
    (including implementing emergency Pass/Fail schemes), reschedule or cancel
    examination windows, and implement extraordinary operational directives to
    guarantee the safety of the academic community while upholding academic
    integrity.
